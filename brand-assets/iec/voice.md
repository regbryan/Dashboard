## 4. Voice Pillars

These are the four traits the IG content actually demonstrates — every caption and graphic should hit at least 2 of these.

### 1. **Plain-spoken Expert**
They explain HVAC the way a good neighbor who happens to be a tech would explain it — no jargon, no scare tactics, no acronyms without context.

> *"If your furnace or heating unit doesn't have routine maintenance, you may need bigger repairs. Heating repairs may not be as expensive as you think, and can be fixed very quickly."*
> — IG post, Dependable Riverside Furnace Repair

### 2. **Walk-You-Through Reassurance**
The brand's signature move is the phrase "walk you through." They sell calm. They don't pressure.

> *"Our expert technicians work with you to make sure to find the heart of the problem, and walk you through the steps of the suggested solution."*

### 3. **Proof Over Promises**
Trust comes from third parties — Google reviews, BBB, named customers. The brand doesn't say "we're the best"; it lets Eric L. say it.

> *"Provided Outstanding Customer Service. Showed Up At Scheduled Time, Explained In Detail Work That Was Needed, And Charged A Fair Price. Highly Recommend!" — Eric L.*

### 4. **Educational First, Sales Second**
Roughly half the IG content is teach-not-sell: home inspection explainers, "how to check if your heater is working efficiently," HVAC system diagrams. Sales lives downstream of the education.

## 6. Tone-by-Context Matrix

| Context | Tone | Example move |
|---|---|---|
| **Educational post (how-to, explainer)** | Patient teacher | Lead with the question the homeowner is actually asking. End with "if you're not sure, give us a call." |
| **Service promo (tune-up, repair special)** | Reassuring expert | State the problem in plain terms, then the fix, then the price range. Never lead with the discount. |
| **Customer review repost** | Quietly proud | Let the quote do the work. One-line caption max. Don't editorialize. |
| **Emergency/24-hour reminder** | Calm, not alarmist | "It's hot. We answer at 2 a.m. Call when you need us." Don't manufacture panic. |
| **Seasonal (spring tune-up, fall furnace check)** | Helpful neighbor | Treat it like a friendly nudge, not a sales push. |
| **Reply to a complaint** | Accountable, no defensiveness | "We're sorry that happened. Can you DM us your address so we can make it right?" |

## 7. Vocabulary

### Words & phrases to use
- "Walk you through"
- "Find the heart of the problem"
- "Expert technicians"
- "Trusted"
- "Dependable"
- "Routine maintenance"
- "Energy-efficient"

- "Fair price"
- "What to expect"
- "Riverside" / "Inland Empire" (claim the territory in headlines)
- "5-star" / "500+ reviews"
- "BBB A+"

### Words to avoid
- "Cheap" (use "fair price" or "honest pricing")
- "Crisis" / "disaster" / "emergency!!" (calm, not alarmist — even for 24-hour service)
- "Cutting-edge" / "next-gen" (too marketing-y)
- "Synergy," "solutions provider," "best-in-class" (corporate jargon)
- "HVAC" without context for first-time customers (spell it out: "heating and air conditioning")
- "Hurry," "limited time" (high-pressure language)
- Any acronym without a one-line explanation (SEER, BTU, MERV)

| **IEC Gold/Yellow** | `#F7CF21` | **Use sparingly.** Reserved for rare highlight bars or callouts only. **Not the default star color, not the default pill color, not the default accent.** Default to red first. |
| **Warm White** | `#FFFFFF` | Clean backgrounds, white text on navy |
| **Cream** | `#EFE9F0` | Warm off-white background for light-mode cards |
| **Dark Brown** | `#463939` | Body text only |

### Color role discipline (hard rules)
1. **Navy `#19437B` is the hero** — every post should lean on it as the dominant color.
2. **Red `#DB2227` is the default accent** — stars, numerals, CTAs, urgency pills. Use red before reaching for any other accent.
3. **Yellow `#F7CF21` is restricted** — use only for rare highlight bars. Never as the default for stars, pills, or buttons.
4. **Teal `#68CCD1` is restricted further** — tiny decorative accents only, never a fill.
5. **Light blue `#97B5CF` pairs with navy** — use for soft secondary backgrounds or info cards to add depth without reaching for teal or yellow.
6. **Before assigning any color to any element, it must map to one of the roles above.** If the guideline doesn't cover it, stop and ask.

### Typography
- **Display / headlines:** Oswald (bold, condensed, all-caps works well)
- **Body:** Poppins or Source Sans Pro (clean, friendly sans)
- **Labels / UI:** Montserrat

### Photography & graphic style (from the 7 uploaded posts)
- **Real over stock when possible** — actual hands, actual furnaces, actual homes
- **Lifestyle shots welcome** — the dog-on-radiator post is on-brand because it's warm and human
- **Diagrams + cutaway illustrations** for explainer content (cool ductwork diagram in Screenshot 4)
- **Logo placement:** patriotic shield logo, usually top-left or top-center, on a white or branded blue field
- **Layout pattern:** Big block headline (Oswald, all caps) + short body paragraph + photo on right OR bottom
- **Background colors that recur:** soft lavender/cream, branded teal, warm white. Avoid pure black backgrounds.

### Photo treatment — curved divider (the IEC signature)
Observed across all 7 reference Instagram posts: the photo does **not** sit inside a plain rectangle. Instead, its left edge is a **soft S-curve** that sweeps gently into the text area, creating the visual signature of an IEC post.

- **Photo column:** right half of the 1080×1080 canvas, approximately 510px wide
- **Right edge of photo:** flush with the canvas edge (straight)
- **Top and bottom edges:** flush with canvas edges (straight)
- **Left edge of photo:** curved — an S-curve that:
  - starts at top-left of the photo column (flush)
  - sweeps slightly *right* into the photo at ~20% down
  - sweeps slightly *left* (toward the text) at ~50–65% down
  - returns to flush at the bottom-left
- **Why it matters:** this curve is what makes the post feel hand-designed and on-brand rather than a stock template. Removing it makes the post feel generic.
- **Implementation note:** in Figma this is a `Vector` node with a custom `vectorPaths` definition, not a clipped rectangle.

### Placeholders (for work-in-progress templates)
- When a photo is not yet dropped in, use a **neutral gray fill (`#E5E5E5`)** with a dashed mid-gray stroke and a `DROP PHOTO HERE` label
- **Never use brand colors as placeholder fills** — teal, red, navy, and gold are reserved for finished-design roles and must not be used as "filler"
