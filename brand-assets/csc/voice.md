## 1. Brand Overview

Cyber Safety Cop is the nation's leading parent-facing brand for online child safety, founded in 2012 by Clayton "Clay" Cranford — a 20+ year law enforcement veteran, former School Resource Officer, juvenile investigator, behavioral threat assessor, and recipient of the 2015 National Bullying Prevention Award. The parent company is Total Safety Solutions, LLC, but "Cyber Safety Cop" is the consumer-facing brand built around Clay's expertise and reputation.

The brand sits at the intersection of *authority* and *empathy*. It is run by a former cop, but it does not feel like a police station. It feels like a calm expert dad pulling parents aside at the school pickup line and saying *"Here's what I've actually seen happen — and here's exactly what to do about it."* The visual identity is sunny yellow, electric blue, and a star-badge logo — bright, hopeful, and trustworthy rather than alarmist. Content is heavy on educational listicles, app reviews, and webinar promos, with periodic brand-deal co-branding (currently Bright Canary).

The differentiator is **earned authority + practical clarity**. Other online safety brands either fear-monger or wave hands. Cyber Safety Cop tells you which app to be worried about today, why, and exactly what to do — in 30 seconds, in plain English, with the calm of someone who has worked the actual cases.

## 4. Voice Pillars

**Pillar 1 — Authoritative without being preachy.**
Clay speaks with the calm certainty of someone who has worked the cases, not someone who read a study. He cites his experience without bragging. Visual headlines like *"TELL. BLOCK. DELETE."* land because they're commands from someone who has earned the right to give them.

**Pillar 2 — Educator, not alarmist.**
Even when the topic is dark (predators, cyberbullying, addiction), the framing is hopeful and equipping. Headlines like *"8 Effects of Screen Time on Children — Have you ever wondered how screen time affects your child's brain?"* lead with curiosity, not panic. The brand never weaponizes fear.

**Pillar 3 — Practical and action-first.**
Every post answers *"so what do I do?"* — set this control, have this conversation, watch for this sign, block this app. Listicles dominate (*"3 Ways to Keep Screen Time Boundaries Clear,"* *"8 Effects..."*). The reader leaves with a TODO, not just a worry.

**Pillar 4 — Warm, parent-to-parent.**
Despite the law enforcement background, the voice is parent talking to parent. "Reclaiming Connection: Breaking Free from Digital Addiction as a Family" is not police-speak — it's family-speak. Clay's appearances on parent podcasts and webinars reinforce this register.

## 7. Vocabulary

**Use (on-brand)**
- Parents, kids, teens, families, our kids
- Screen time, parental controls, digital citizenship
- Safe, smart, healthy, equipped, empowered, confident
- "What you need to know," "here's what to do," "in plain English"
- App names, specific platform features (always say *which* app)
- "After 20 years on the job…", "in my experience…"

**Avoid (off-brand)**
- "Predator hysteria" framing — say "online risks" not "predators around every corner"
- Tech jargon without translation (don't say "OAuth" without explaining)
- Cop jargon ("perpetrator," "suspect," "officer responded")
- Doom phrases ("the kids are not okay," "we're losing them," "it's already too late")
- Generic platitudes ("technology is a tool," "communication is key" — without a what-to-do)
- Hard sell language ("buy now," "limited time," "act fast")

**Define on first use** (technical jargon parents may not know)
- *Ephemeral messaging* — messages that auto-delete, like Snapchat or Instagram disappearing photos
- *EXIF data* — hidden location info embedded in photos
- *Dox / doxxing* — publishing someone's personal info online
- *Catfishing* — pretending to be someone else online
- *Sextortion* — being blackmailed with intimate images
- *Behavioral threat assessment* — the structured process schools use to evaluate concerning behavior

### Typography

- **Display headline:** Bold sans-serif (heavy weight, often condensed), ALL CAPS or sentence case, navy blue or yellow on contrasting background. Examples: *"8 EFFECTS"*, *"DOES YOUR CHILD USE THIS APP?"*, *"TELL. BLOCK. DELETE."*
- **Subhead / supporting:** Medium-weight sans-serif, navy on yellow or white on navy.
- **Body / detail:** Regular sans-serif, navy or white, generous line height for parent readability.
- **Logo wordmark:** Semibold sans, "CYBER SAFETY COP" with smaller "TOTAL SAFETY SOLUTIONS, LLC" tagline beneath, paired with star-badge icon.

### Photography direction

- **Real kids and parents in real situations** — phone on couch, talking on the floor, parent looking concerned but engaged
- **Natural light, clean backgrounds, contemporary clothing**
- **Costumed/playful** for membership and aspirational posts (kid in superhero cape, jumping)
- **Phone-screen hero shots** for app review posts (single phone front-facing, app icon visible, white or color background)
- **AVOID:** stock-photo "shocked mom holding head" cliché, dramatic over-staged lighting, dark/scary scenes
