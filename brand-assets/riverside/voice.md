> **Status:** v1, evidence-based. Built from kit.md + 7 reference-posts on @riversidehatco. Visual voice strong (custom-hat product photography, customer-with-hat selfies, Hat Bar trailer). Caption voice inferred — drop a `captions.md` to upgrade.

## 1. Brand Overview

Riverside Hat Co is a small-batch Western hat shop in Riverside, CA — founded 2021 — known for **custom hat shaping** and the **Hat Bar Trailer** (mobile pop-up). The visual identity is unmistakably Western but **not costume-y**: black felt cowboys, classic creases, leather goods on rich dark backgrounds, real customers wearing real hats.

The brand sells **personal style + craftsmanship**, not Halloween dress-up. Every hat looks like it belongs on someone heading to a real ranch, a Stagecoach Festival, or a wedding party — not a theme park.

## 2. Audience

- **Primary:** Inland Empire locals 18-45, Western-fashion-curious. Country-music adjacent. Country Thunder / Stagecoach attendees. Folks who want a hat that says something about them.
- **Secondary:** **Brides + bridal parties** for wedding-day custom hats — confirmed via gift-certificate styling and the Hat Bar Trailer's event-booking framing.
- **Tertiary:** Event bookings — businesses, festivals, rodeos that hire the Hat Bar Trailer as a mobile activation.
- **Quaternary:** Customers buying hat-shaping or hat-cleaning services for hats they already own.

## 3. Voice Pillars

### 1. **Confidently casual**
"You came to the right hat shop." Not stiff, not bro-y. Talks to customers like a friendly hat expert who's also kind of cool. The Hat Bar Trailer image (founder leaning on the trailer with a knowing nod) sets the tone — relaxed, certain, unpretentious.

### 2. **Western, not costume**
Cowboys, but real cowboys. References ranches, rodeos, weddings, festivals, country concerts. **Doesn't reference:** "yeehaw," "howdy partner," cosplay tropes, dated rhinestone-country aesthetics. The brand wears Western the way Yellowstone wears Western — modern, lived-in, slightly hardened.

### 3. **Craft-forward**
The hat is a small piece of craftsmanship. Photos lean into the curve of the brim, the shape of the crown, the way the felt sits. Captions should name what was done ("re-creased the crown for a taller silhouette," "swapped the band for distressed brown") instead of just "new hat."

### 4. **Local pride**
Riverside CA / Inland Empire identity is part of the brand. Phone number (951.966.6912) painted on the trailer = local-business posture. EST. 2021 = recent enough to still be telling the founding story.

## 5. Tone-by-Context Matrix

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| New hat drop / Hat Bar event | Confident, anticipation-building. "We brought the bar to you." |
| Custom-shape transformation post | Process-respecting. "Re-shaped + re-banded." Show the change. |
| Customer feature | Warm, name-the-customer-by-first-name, celebrate the look |
| Care tip / how-to | Practical, expert-friend, never condescending |
| Sale / promo | Direct, dollar-amount-clear, no FOMO manipulation |
| Wedding-party content | Romantic-but-grounded. Names of the couple, the date stamped inside the band, the story |

## 6. Vocabulary

### Words & phrases to use
- "Shaped," "re-creased," "re-banded" (instead of "modified")
- "Brim," "crown," "sweat band," "ribbon," "feathers"
- "Hat Bar," "trailer," "pop-up"
- "Custom," "one-of-one," "yours"
- "Ranch," "rodeo," "festival" — places real Western hats go
- "Felt," "straw," "panama," "palm-leaf" — name the material
- "[Name] came in for…" — personalize customer features
- Local references: "Riverside," "Inland Empire," "the IE"

### Words to avoid
- "Yeehaw," "howdy," "partner" (as in "howdy partner") — costume cowboy
- "Country boy/girl" — cliché
- "Vibes" — overused, vague
- "Statement piece" — fashion-magazine generic
- "Authentic" — show, don't claim
- "Crafted with care" / "made with love" — empty signal
- Em dashes as connective tissue in captions (use periods, commas)

## 7. Visual Identity

Confirmed from reference-posts:

- **Product photography:** Hats on rich, dark backgrounds — black felt against a dark wood credenza, leather-textured drawer fronts, rope, tooled leather. Warm side-light, shallow depth of field.
- **Customer portraits:** Half-bust shots, customer in their new hat, sunglasses on (mood), often with a small smirk or knowing look. NOT polished studio — looks like real moments captured fast.
- **Trailer / location shots:** Founder/team leaning on the Hat Bar Trailer with the brand mark + phone number visible. Outdoor light, casual posture.
- **Promo graphics (gift certificates, drops):** Wooden plank background, red ribbon, brand mark in oval. Warm earthy palette throughout.

### Locked palette (per `brand.json`)
- Primary (warm tan): `#B89A6D`
- Secondary (dark saddle brown): `#3A2E1F`
- Accent (rust): `#C9572C`
- Background (cream): `#F2E6D5`

No off-palette colors. Particularly: **no bright blue, no neon, no pastels, no jewel tones.**

## 8. Photography style

- Real customers (or staff stand-ins) > stock models
- Hat in context (on a person, on a wall, in the Hat Bar, on a counter) > floating product shots
- Warm color grading (golden hour, slight orange shift)
- Imperfection is fine — a hand-cropped phone photo of a returning customer outperforms a polished studio shot

## 13. Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet

- **Platform:** Instagram, 2x/week W/Sat
- **Format:** Image-first (60%), Reel (40%) — visual brand, reels-heavy
- **Voice:** Confidently casual, Western-not-costume, craft-forward, locally proud
- **Visual:** Warm earthy palette, real-customer portraits, hat in context
- **Hashtags:** 3-7 per post, identity + product + style + event mix
- **Tone test:** If it sounds like the Stagecoach version of Levi's, it's right. If it sounds like the Halloween-store version of "country," rewrite.
